# Historical research

## Albert Abrams

Abrams' 1922 edition of *New Concepts in Diagnosis and Treatment* describes his claimed Electronic Reactions of Abrams. He interpreted abdominal percussion responses through instrument settings and proposed that the Oscilloclast acted through resonance. These are historical assertions, not accepted project mechanisms. [S01, printed pp. 49–51 and 383]

His diagnostic “vibration rates” include resistance readings in ohms. Ohms are not hertz. This project has established no validated conversion from those readings to an audio frequency. Diagnostic instruments and treatment apparatus also need to be distinguished. [S01; engineering interpretation]

The Smithsonian holds an Oscilloclast and references Samuel O. Hoffman's patent US1445951A. That patent describes periodic switching, giving an example near 200 interruptions per minute, approximately 3.33 per second. This is a component switching rate, not a complete output specification or disease-specific treatment frequency. [S02, S03]

A contemporary Nature account reports that the Scientific American investigation did not substantiate Abrams' claims. Critical findings belong in the record alongside proponents' claims. [S04]

## Rife-associated devices

We have not yet established a primary-source reconstruction of Rife's apparatus, its revisions, or a documented circuit relationship to Abrams' devices. Do not assert technical equivalence without evidence. INV-02 tracks this investigation.

## Modern commercial machines

Owner photographs identify an Electro-Med 235p with Natural Health branding [S11]. Its manufacturer/revision and electrical specifications remain unverified. Its output cannot be inferred from appearance or the Rife label. See the [machine investigation](Existing-Machine-Investigation.md); INV-01 and INV-03 remain open for documentation and characterization.

## Awaiting review
